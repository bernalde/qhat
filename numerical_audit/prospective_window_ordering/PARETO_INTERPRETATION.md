# Interpreting the cost/error frontiers

The primary archive retains the predeclared frontier rule: exact recorded cost comparison and an absolute physical-error ambiguity band of 10⁻¹². Its original data and memberships remain unchanged. Frontier membership is descriptive for a fixed case, repetition, time and rotation budget; it is not a measurement-uncertainty or optimality certificate.

Recorded CPU sums can differ by one binary64 rounding unit even when they represent the same underlying time-counter total. For example, `0.1` and `0.09999999999999999` change strict-cost dominance in the first repetition of `BeH2_r1p907_631g_14m4e` (primary groups 24–27). Such a difference is not meaningful evidence of a faster construction.

The separately labelled [postreview CPU-cost sensitivity view](evidence/review_resolution_2026-10-29/CPU_PARETO_SENSITIVITY.json) applies a fixed absolute cost tolerance of 10⁻¹² seconds to all 32 groups and every one of their 11 paths. It keeps the original physical-error tolerance, measurements, statuses and strong controls. A point dominates another only if its cost is no greater within the cost tolerance, its error is no greater within the error tolerance, and at least one difference exceeds its corresponding tolerance. This is an additive interpretation check, not replacement of the frozen primary analysis or retuning of training. Resource-admission and accounting ceilings remain strict. An [independent exact-arithmetic check](evidence/review_resolution_2026-10-29/CPU_PARETO_SENSITIVITY_CHECK.json) verifies the supplemental memberships and complete changed-group inventory.

`unscored_no_numeric_claim` describes a training-objective claim, not missing physical validation. The canonical, magnitude and current baselines have independently validated physical errors and remain legitimate strong controls. The `verified` label on first-response, Pauli, cubic and window paths refers to their checked claimed objective. Filtering the physical cost/error comparison to objective-verified paths would discard valid baselines. Both labels remain explicit in the complete point records.

At T=0.4 and the 24U budget, differences between several second-order errors lie inside the declared absolute 10⁻¹² band. This can make cheaper baselines dominate trained paths, including some errors just above the floor. A large percentage ratio near that floor does not establish a resolved difference under the declared absolute tolerance. Keep the complete error values and floor flags when interpreting those memberships.

The cost axis measures one whole ordering-construction call shared by its validation times, budgets and applicable formula variants. It excludes subsequent propagation and quantum-circuit execution. Reusing that cost on multiple rows does not create multiple charged construction calls. Short-family wall measurements cluster around the guard's roughly three-second process/polling floor; millisecond changes can alter a single repetition's wall frontier. They do not establish a reproducible fast-family ranking. The report's aggregate construction-cost table and both recorded repetitions provide the broader context. The CPU sensitivity view copies the original wall frontiers without claiming that their timing noise has been removed.
