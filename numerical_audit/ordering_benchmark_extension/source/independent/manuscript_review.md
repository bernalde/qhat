## Actionable findings

1. **Moderate — distinguish demonstrated search benefit from an unisolated partition benefit.** `theory/expanded_benchmark_evidence/findings.tex:14–18` says weighting has a smaller demonstrated role than “the refined partition and coherent insertion search”; `main.tex:289–293` similarly says the panel supports “the partition and search.” The same-partition magnitude control isolates a benefit from search. The JD/J0 control isolates the weighting. Neither isolates the value of splitting D, because there is no otherwise matched JD search with the complete unsplit diagonal unit. The complete-class/refined-label Strang equivalence explicitly proves that refinement alone does not change this same-order formula. Suggested wording: “The added diagonal weighting has a smaller demonstrated effect than coherent insertion search within the refined conserving partition. These controls do not separately measure the gain from making diagonal terms independently movable.” No additional experiment is necessary if the causal claim is narrowed this way.

2. **Moderate — name the stronger symmetric controls instead of generalizing over symmetric formulas.** `main.tex:41–43` says symmetric formulas outperform the first-order candidate at fine rotation budgets, and `main.tex:325` says they “are stronger at fine budgets.” The detailed results correctly report that conserving-order and complete-class-label Strang win all 14 at 100U, and magnitude-Pauli Strang wins 12 with two floor ties. However, signed-Pauli Strang loses to the first-order candidate on four of the six prospective follow-ups at 100U (`optimized/REPORT.md`). Suggested abstract/conclusion wording: “Conserving-order and complete-class-label Strang outperform the first-order candidate on all fourteen cases at the finest tested budget.” If space is tight, “Several symmetric controls outperform…” avoids an unsupported family-wide reading. Retain the existing distinction between first-order score optimization and empirical symmetric transfer.

Use `Labels use \texttt{g} for geometry scaling and \texttt{c} for coefficient scaling`. This is independent of the pending figure generation.

4. **Low — clarify what coefficient scaling tests.** After `theory/expanded_ordering_benchmarks.tex:10–13`, add that multiplying a frozen Hamiltonian by alpha at T=1 is dynamically equivalent to using the original Hamiltonian at time alpha. If schedules are regenerated for that scaled target, this is not a test of transfer of one unchanged ordering across times.
